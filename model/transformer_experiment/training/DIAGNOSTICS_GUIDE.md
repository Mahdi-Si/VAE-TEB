git st# Training Diagnostics Plot Guide

This document explains the diagnostic figure generated by `TransformerPlotCallback` during training. One figure is saved every `plot_frequency` epochs for each of `num_examples` validation samples. Files are saved to `<output_dir>/transformer_diagnostics/` and optionally logged as MLflow artifacts.

The figure has **11 rows** when raw FHR/UP signals are available in the batch (i.e. `fhr` and `up` are in `load_fields`), or **10 rows** otherwise. Each figure is a single column of wide subplots, providing a complete snapshot of the model's internal state at that training epoch.

---

## Row 0 (conditional) -- Raw FHR and UP Signals

**When it appears:** Only when the dataloader includes the raw `fhr` and `up` fields (set `load_fields: [fhr_st, up_st, fhr, up]` in `config.yaml`). If raw signals are not in the batch, this row is skipped and the figure starts at the FHR-ST heatmap.

**What it shows:** The raw FHR signal (blue, left y-axis, in bpm) and raw UP signal (green, right y-axis, in mmHg) plotted as time-series over the full 20-minute window. This is the physiological signal before any scattering transform or normalization.

**How to read it:**
- Horizontal axis = time in seconds (0 to ~1200s for a 20-minute window).
- Left y-axis (blue) = FHR in beats per minute. Normal baseline is ~110--160 bpm.
- Right y-axis (green) = uterine pressure in mmHg. Contractions appear as peaks.

**What to look for:**
- **FHR baseline**: Should be in the 110--160 bpm range. Sustained tachycardia (>160) or bradycardia (<110) are clinically significant.
- **Decelerations**: Dips in FHR following contractions (early/late decelerations) or independent of contractions (variable decelerations).
- **Variability**: The FHR trace should show beat-to-beat variability. A flat trace suggests reduced variability (a concerning sign).
- **Contraction pattern**: Regular UP peaks indicate active labor. The timing relationship between UP peaks and FHR dips is the core of what the model tries to learn.
- **Data quality**: Flat regions or sudden jumps suggest signal dropout or sensor issues.

This plot provides clinical context for interpreting all subsequent rows. The scattering transform (next rows) extracts multiscale features from these raw signals.

## Row 1 -- FHR Scattering Transform (Input)

**What it shows:** A heatmap of the input FHR scattering-transform coefficients for this 20-minute window. Shape is `(43 channels, 300 time steps)`. Channel 0 is the zeroth-order scattering coefficient (smoothed energy); channels 1--42 are first-order wavelet coefficients at different scales. Colourmap is blue-white-red (`bwr`), centred at zero.

**How to read it:**
- Horizontal axis = time (300 steps, each ~4 seconds, spanning 20 minutes).
- Vertical axis = scattering channel index (low frequencies at top, higher at bottom).
- Bright red/blue bands indicate strong scattering energy at that scale and time.

**What to look for:**
- Vertical stripes across many channels indicate broadband events (e.g. decelerations).
- Channel 0 should show a smooth baseline with slow drift.
- If the input looks like pure noise or is all-zero, the data pipeline may be broken.

---

## Row 2 -- UP Scattering Transform (Input)

**What it shows:** Same as Row 0, but for the uterine pressure (UP) signal. 43 channels, 300 time steps.

**How to read it:**
- Contraction events appear as localized bright regions in the mid-frequency channels.
- Between contractions, the signal is relatively flat.

**What to look for:**
- Clear contraction patterns (periodic bright vertical bands) indicate normal UP data.
- If this looks identical to Row 0 or is all-zero, the wrong field may have been loaded.

---

## Row 3 -- FHR Encoder States (H_F)

**What it shows:** The output of the FHR-only causal transformer encoder. Shape is `(192 hidden dims, 300 time steps)`. This is the model's learned representation of the FHR signal after the stem convolutions and 4 layers of causal self-attention.

**How to read it:**
- Each row is one hidden dimension. The pattern shows what temporal features that dimension encodes.
- `bwr` colourmap centred at zero; `origin="lower"` so dimension 0 is at the bottom.

**What to look for during training:**
- **Early training (random weights):** Noisy, unstructured pattern with no temporal coherence.
- **Mid training:** Emergence of structured horizontal bands (certain dimensions consistently activate) and vertical patterns aligned with input events.
- **Good training:** Clear temporal structure -- some dimensions respond to baseline changes, others to variability, others to deceleration events.
- **Bad sign:** If this remains noisy after many epochs, the FHR encoder is not learning.

---

## Row 4 -- Fused Encoder States (H_FU)

**What it shows:** The output of the fused causal encoder, which processes the gated combination of FHR and UP information. Shape is `(192, 300)`.

**How to read it:**
- Similar to Row 2, but this representation has been augmented with UP information through the cross-attention fusion layer.

**What to look for:**
- **Difference from Row 2:** If H_FU looks identical to H_F, the fusion is not injecting any UP information. This is expected in very early training but should diverge as the gate learns.
- **Good sign:** H_FU shows additional temporal structure (especially around contraction times visible in Row 1) that is absent in H_F.
- **Correlation with UP events:** Regions of H_FU that differ from H_F should temporally align with UP activity in Row 1.

---

## Row 5 -- Latent Dynamics (PCA of Fused Representation)

**What it shows:** The top 3 principal components of the 192-dim fused encoder states H_FU, plotted as time series over the 300 time steps. Each line is one PC, coloured blue (PC1), orange (PC2), green (PC3). The legend shows the percentage of variance each component explains.

**How to read it:**
- Horizontal axis = time steps (0--300).
- Vertical axis = projection value along each principal component.
- These are the dominant modes of variation in the fused latent representation over the 20-minute window.

**What to look for:**
- **Early training:** PCs are noisy with no temporal coherence. Variance may be spread evenly across many components.
- **Good training:** PC1 captures the dominant temporal trend (baseline drift, overall state). PC2-3 capture secondary patterns (contraction-related oscillations, variability changes). The variance explained by PC1 should increase as the model learns more structured representations.
- **Clinically meaningful structure:** Smooth slow trends in PC1 (baseline), oscillatory patterns in PC2-3 aligned with contraction frequency (visible in Row 2).
- **Flat PCs:** If all components are flat, the model is learning a static representation that doesn't capture temporal dynamics.
- **PC variance concentration:** If PC1 explains >80%, the representation is low-rank (may be fine for simple dynamics but could miss subtle patterns). If variance is spread evenly across many PCs, the representation may be noisy or the model is not yet converging.

---

## Row 6 -- Fusion Contribution (||H_FU - H_F|| over Time)

**What it shows:** Two overlaid traces showing how much the fused representation differs from the FHR-only representation at each time step:
- **Orange (left axis):** L2 norm of (H_FU - H_F) — absolute difference in the 192-dim space.
- **Purple (right axis):** Relative change as a percentage: ||H_FU - H_F|| / ||H_F|| * 100.
- Red dotted vertical lines mark anchor positions.

**How to read it:**
- High values = large fusion contribution at that time step (UP information substantially changes the representation).
- Low values = the fused representation is nearly identical to FHR-only (UP adds little).

**What to look for:**
- **Early training:** Roughly uniform small difference (random weights, gate near 0.5).
- **Good training:** Peaks in the orange line should align with contraction events (visible in Row 2). This means the model learns to inject UP information specifically when contractions are happening — which is physiologically correct.
- **Correlation with gate (Row 7):** The fusion contribution should correlate with gate activation. High gate → high ||H_FU - H_F||. If the gate is high but the difference is small, the cross-attention may not be producing useful context.
- **Mean value in title:** Shows the average fusion contribution. If this is very close to zero after many epochs, the model isn't using UP information.
- **Relative change:** If the absolute difference is large but relative change is tiny (<1%), the FHR representation itself has very large norms and the UP contribution may be drowned out.

---

## Row 7 -- Mean Gate Activation (UP -> FHR Influence)

**What it shows:** The fusion gate value averaged across all 192 hidden dimensions, plotted as a line over the 300 time steps. The shaded band shows +/-1 standard deviation across dimensions. Red dotted vertical lines mark anchor positions used for prediction.

**How to read it:**
- The gate value is in `[0, 1]`. A value of 0 means "ignore UP completely"; 1 means "fully incorporate UP context".
- The dashed horizontal line at 0.5 marks the midpoint.

**What to look for:**
- **Early training:** The gate often hovers near 0.5 (uninformative sigmoid output from random weights).
- **Good training:** The gate becomes temporally modulated -- rising during/after contractions (when UP information is predictive of FHR response) and falling between contractions (when FHR is self-sufficient).
- **Collapsed gate (always ~0 or always ~1):** The model is either ignoring UP entirely or unconditionally injecting it. This suggests a learning problem.
- **Gate-contraction alignment:** Compare peaks in Row 4 to contraction events in Row 1. In a well-trained model, gate activity should correlate with contraction timing.
- **Standard deviation band:** A narrow band means all dimensions agree on the gate value; a wide band means different dimensions gate differently (which is healthy -- some features are UP-dependent, others are not).

---

## Row 8 -- Forecast at Anchor (Self vs Fused vs TE vs Target)

**What it shows:** A time-series plot zoomed into one anchor point, showing the ground-truth future FHR (channel 0) and predictions from all three heads:
- **Black line:** Ground truth future FHR scattering coefficient (channel 0).
- **Blue line:** Self-only head prediction (uses only FHR history).
- **Orange line:** Fused head prediction (uses FHR + UP history).
- **Green line:** TE-augmented prediction (`stop_grad(self) + TE_residual`).

Shaded regions mark:
- **Purple:** Context window (L_ctx = 30 steps before the anchor).
- **Red dashed line:** Anchor position.
- **Gray:** Guard gap (4 steps between anchor and prediction start).

**How to read it:**
- The prediction lines should approach the black ground-truth line as training progresses.
- The x-axis spans from the context window through the guard gap to the prediction horizon (h=30 steps).

**What to look for:**
- **Early training:** All three predictions are roughly flat or random -- far from the ground truth.
- **Good training (Stage 1):** Self-only and fused lines approach the ground truth. Fused should be slightly better.
- **Good training (Stage 2+):** The green TE-augmented line should differ from blue where UP provides extra predictive information.
- **Fused vs Self-only gap:** If the orange (fused) line is consistently closer to ground truth than blue (self), the model is successfully using UP information.
- **TE residual effect:** The green-blue difference represents what the TE latent adds. In Stage 1 (lambda_te=0), these are identical.

---

## Row 9 -- Mean Absolute Error by Horizon and Head

**What it shows:** A grouped bar chart comparing MAE for the three prediction heads (Self-only, Fused, TE-augmented) across all three horizons (h=8, h=15, h=30 time steps, corresponding to ~32s, ~60s, ~120s).

**How to read it:**
- Three groups on the x-axis (one per horizon). Three bars per group (one per head).
- Lower bars = better prediction.

**What to look for:**
- **Horizon trend:** Error should increase with horizon (longer-term prediction is harder). If h=30 error is lower than h=8, something is wrong.
- **Head ordering:** Ideally: `Fused <= TE-aug < Self-only`. If self-only beats fused, the model isn't benefiting from UP information yet.
- **Convergence:** Across epochs, all bars should decrease. If one head's bars stop decreasing while others continue, that head may be under-trained or over-regularized.
- **Stage 1 vs Stage 2:** In Stage 1, TE-aug bars equal self-only bars (TE branch inactive). In Stage 2+, TE-aug should be between self-only and fused.

---

## Row 10 -- TE Latent: Posterior vs Prior Means at Anchors

**What it shows:** A split heatmap. The bottom half shows posterior means (mu) from `q(z|FHR, UP)` and the top half shows prior means (mu_0) from `r(z|FHR)`, both plotted as `(d_z=16 dimensions, K anchors)`. A white dashed line separates the two halves.

**How to read it:**
- Horizontal axis = anchor index (the K anchors used for prediction).
- Vertical axis = latent dimensions (0--15 for posterior, 16--31 for prior).
- `bwr` colourmap centred at zero.

**What to look for:**
- **Posterior = Prior (identical top and bottom halves):** The TE latent carries no source-incremental information. This is expected in Stage 1 (TE not trained) and early Stage 2.
- **Posterior differs from Prior:** The model has learned that UP provides information beyond FHR alone. The **difference** between the two halves is the "extra" information from UP.
- **Structured patterns:** If certain latent dimensions consistently differ between posterior and prior across anchors, those dimensions encode specific UP-to-FHR coupling patterns.
- **All zeros or collapsed to a single value:** The latent has collapsed (posterior variance too small or KL too strong).

---

## Row 11 -- KL Divergence per Anchor

**What it shows:** A bar chart of the total KL divergence `KL(posterior || prior)` at each anchor position. The title shows the current beta weight.

**How to read it:**
- Each bar represents how much extra information the posterior encodes compared to the prior at that anchor. Higher KL = more source-incremental information captured.

**What to look for:**
- **Stage 1 (beta=0, lambda_te=0):** KL values are meaningless -- the TE branch is not trained.
- **Stage 2 (beta=0, lambda_te=0.25):** KL should be non-zero and growing as the TE branch learns. Since beta=0, there's no KL penalty yet, so KL can grow freely.
- **Stage 3 (beta ramping):** KL should stabilize or decrease as the beta penalty kicks in. If KL drops to near-zero everywhere, the bottleneck is too aggressive (beta_max too high). If KL remains very large, beta_max is too small.
- **Variation across anchors:** Higher KL at certain anchors suggests those time points have stronger UP-to-FHR coupling (e.g. during/after contractions). This is the expected behavior.
- **Uniform KL across anchors:** Either the coupling is uniform (unlikely physiologically) or the model hasn't learned to discriminate.

---

## Row 12 -- Window Embedding (e_win) Component Magnitudes

**What it shows:** A bar chart of the absolute activation magnitudes of the full window embedding vector (1568 dimensions), colour-coded by component:
- **Blue (dims 0--383):** `e_F` -- intrinsic FHR summary (attention pool + max pool of H_F).
- **Orange (dims 384--1535):** `e_FU` -- fused multimodal summary (attention pool + max pool + 4 quarter mean pools of H_FU).
- **Green (dims 1536--1567):** `e_TE` -- transfer entropy summary (mean + max of TE posterior means).

Vertical dashed lines mark the boundaries between components.

**How to read it:**
- This is the representation that will be used for downstream classification. Its structure matters for transfer quality.

**What to look for:**
- **Balanced magnitudes:** All three sections (blue, orange, green) should have meaningful non-zero activations. If one section is all-zero while others are active, that component is dead.
- **e_TE (green) magnitude:** In Stage 1, the green section may be near-zero or random. In Stage 2+, it should develop non-trivial values. If it remains flat after Stage 2, the TE branch is not learning.
- **e_F vs e_FU difference:** If the blue and orange sections look identical, the fusion is not adding information to the representation.
- **Sparsity:** Some sparsity in the embedding is normal (not all dimensions active for every sample). But if >50% of dimensions are near-zero, the representation may be under-utilized.
- **Scale consistency:** If one section has magnitudes 100x larger than another, the downstream classifier may ignore the smaller section. This suggests a normalization issue.

## Tracking Training Progress Across Epochs

By comparing the same sample across multiple epochs, you can track:

| Signal | What to watch |
|--------|---------------|
| Row 0 raw FHR/UP context | Clinical context for interpretation |
| Rows 3-4 evolving from noise to structure | Encoder learning |
| Row 5 PCA components becoming smooth and structured | Latent dynamics emerging |
| Row 6 fusion contribution peaks aligning with contractions | UP information being used spatially |
| Row 7 gate becoming temporally modulated | Fusion learning |
| Row 8 predictions approaching ground truth | Forecast quality improving |
| Row 9 bars decreasing, fused < self | UP information quantitatively improving forecasts |
| Row 10 posterior diverging from prior | TE latent becoming informative |
| Row 11 KL stabilizing under beta pressure | Bottleneck working |
| Row 12 all three sections active | Complete representation |

### Stage Transition Checkpoints

- **End of Stage 1:** Rows 3-4 should show structure. Row 5 PCA should show smooth temporal trends. Row 6 should show non-zero fusion contribution. Row 8/9 should show improving forecasts. Row 7 should show some gate modulation. Rows 10-11 are uninformative (TE not active).
- **End of Stage 2:** Row 10 should show posterior-prior divergence. Row 11 should show non-zero KL. Row 9 should show TE-aug between self and fused.
- **End of Stage 3:** Row 11 KL should be stabilized. Row 12 green section should be active. Row 9 gaps between heads should be consistent.
