# Extending $\beta$-TCVAE to the Sequential VAE TEB Model

This document outlines how to adapt and implement the $\beta$-Total Correlation VAE ($\beta$-TCVAE) framework within our existing `SeqVaeTeb` model. The goal is to learn a disentangled latent representation $\mathbf{z}$ of the target Fetal Heart Rate (FHR) signal, which can lead to more interpretable models and improved downstream task performance.

---

## 1. Core Concepts Recap

### SeqVae-TEB
- **Objective**: Models a target signal $\mathbf{y}$ (FHR features) conditioned on a source signal $\mathbf{x}$ (UP features).
- **Core Loss Term**: Minimizes $\text{KL}(q(\mathbf{z}|\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}) \| p(\mathbf{z}|\mathbf{y}))$, which acts as a proxy for the Transfer Entropy from source to target.
- **Latent Variable**: $\mathbf{z}$ is sampled from the posterior $q(\mathbf{z}|\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})$.
- **Prior**: The prior $p(\mathbf{z}|\mathbf{y})$ is **conditional and learned**, its parameters are generated by the `TargetEncoder` based on the input $\mathbf{y}$.

### $\beta$-TCVAE
- **Objective**: Learns disentangled representations by penalizing statistical dependencies between latent dimensions.
- **Core Loss Term**: Decomposes the standard VAE KL-divergence $\text{KL}(q(\mathbf{z}|n) \| p(\mathbf{z}))$ into three components:
  1.  **Index-Code Mutual Information (MI)**: $I(\mathbf{z};n)$
  2.  **Total Correlation (TC)**: $\text{TC}(\mathbf{z})$
  3.  **Dimension-wise KL**: $\sum_j \text{KL}(q(z_j) \| p(z_j))$
- **Key Insight**: Penalizing the Total Correlation term $\text{TC}(\mathbf{z})$ is the primary driver for disentanglement.
- **Prior**: The prior $p(\mathbf{z})$ is **fixed and simple**, typically a standard normal distribution $\mathcal{N}(0, \mathbf{I})$.

---

## 2. Merging TEB and $\beta$-TCVAE: Two Philosophical Approaches

The core challenge in combining these two frameworks is reconciling their different approaches to the prior distribution. The TEB framework uses a complex, learned, conditional prior $p(\mathbf{z}|\mathbf{y})$, while $\beta$-TCVAE relies on a simple, fixed prior $p(\mathbf{z}) = \mathcal{N}(0, \mathbf{I})$ to achieve its disentanglement objective. 

Below are two distinct approaches to this integration.

### Approach 1: Full $\beta$-TCVAE Integration (Prioritizing Disentanglement & Generation)

This approach fully adopts the $\beta$-TCVAE objective, replacing the original TEB KL-divergence term entirely. This prioritizes learning a disentangled, generative latent space.

#### 2.1. Mathematical Formulation

The original loss term $\beta \cdot \text{KL}(q(\mathbf{z}|\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}) \| p(\mathbf{z}|\mathbf{y}))$ is replaced by the complete $\beta$-TCVAE decomposition. The new objective function becomes:

$$ 
\mathcal{L} = \mathbb{E}_{q(\mathbf{z}|\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})}[\log p(\text{recon}|\mathbf{z})] + \alpha \cdot I_q(\mathbf{z};n) + \beta \cdot \text{TC}(\mathbf{z}) + \gamma \cdot \sum_j \text{KL}(q(z_j) \| p(z_j)) 
$$ 

- **Reconstruction Loss**: $\log p(\text{recon}|\mathbf{z})$ remains the same (NLL for raw signal + MSE for features).
- **Index-Code MI**: $I_q(\mathbf{z};n) = \mathbb{E}_{q(\mathbf{z},n)} [ \log q(\mathbf{z}|\mathbf{x}_n, \mathbf{y}_n) - \log q(\mathbf{z}) ]$. This term now acts as the primary information bottleneck, encouraging $\mathbf{z}$ to be a compressed representation of the $(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})$ pair.
- **Total Correlation (TC)**: $\text{TC}(\mathbf{z}) = \mathbb{E}_{q(\mathbf{z})}[\log q(\mathbf{z}) - \sum_j \log q(z_j)]$. This is the main disentanglement penalty, forcing the latent dimensions to become statistically independent.
- **Dimension-wise KL**: This term now compares the marginals of the aggregated posterior to the standard normal prior: $\sum_j \text{KL}(q(z_j) \| p(z_j))$, where $p(z_j) = \mathcal{N}(0, 1)$.

#### 2.2. Implications

- **Prior**: The model is now trained to make the aggregated posterior $q(\mathbf{z})$ match the fixed, standard normal prior $p(\mathbf{z}) = \mathcal{N}(0, \mathbf{I})$. The learned, conditional prior $p(\mathbf{z}|\mathbf{y})$ is no longer part of the loss function.
- **Generation**: This model becomes fully generative. To create a new, unconditional sample, you simply:
  1. Draw a latent vector $\mathbf{z}$ from the standard normal prior: $\mathbf{z} \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \mathbf{I})$.
  2. Pass $\mathbf{z}$ to the decoder.
- **Control**: Because the latent space is structured and disentangled, you can traverse individual latent dimensions to achieve controllable generation (e.g., modifying FHR baseline or variability by changing a single value in the $\mathbf{z}$ vector).

**Recommendation**: This is the recommended approach if the primary goal is to create an interpretable, controllable, and generative model of FHR signals. The rest of this document details this implementation.

### Approach 2: Hybrid "Disentangled TEB" Model (Prioritizing the TEB Objective)

This approach seeks to preserve the original TEB objective while adding an explicit penalty for disentanglement.

#### 2.3. Mathematical Formulation

Instead of replacing the KL term, we augment the original loss function with an additional Total Correlation penalty:

$$ 
\mathcal{L} = \mathbb{E}_{q(\mathbf{z}|\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})}[\log p(\text{recon}|\mathbf{z})] + \beta \cdot \text{KL}(q(\mathbf{z}|\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}) \| p(\mathbf{z}|\mathbf{y})) + \gamma \cdot \text{TC}(\mathbf{z}) 
$$ 

- **Reconstruction Loss**: Unchanged.
- **TEB KL-Divergence**: The original term, $\text{KL}(q(\mathbf{z}|\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}) \| p(\mathbf{z}|\mathbf{y}))$, is preserved. The $\beta$ hyperparameter continues to control the information bottleneck from the source $\mathbf{x}$.
- **Total Correlation**: We add a new, independent term $\text{TC}(\mathbf{z})$ to penalize latent variable dependencies. The $\gamma$ hyperparameter controls the strength of this disentanglement pressure.

#### 2.4. Implications

- **Prior**: The prior remains the **conditional, learned prior $p(\mathbf{z}|\mathbf{y})$**. The model is not forced to match a standard normal distribution.
- **Generation**: Generation remains **conditional**. To create a sample, you must first provide a target signal $\mathbf{y}$ to the `TargetEncoder` to get the parameters for $p(\mathbf{z}|\mathbf{y})$. You then sample $\mathbf{z}$ from this distribution and pass it to the decoder. Unconditional generation by sampling from $\mathcal{N}(0, \mathbf{I})$ is not possible.
- **Control**: While you cannot generate samples unconditionally, the latent space of the conditional generator would be more disentangled. This allows for more interpretable analysis of how the latent factors influence the reconstruction for a *given* $\mathbf{y}$.
- **Challenges**: Tuning two separate hyperparameters ($\beta$ for information bottleneck, $\gamma$ for disentanglement) that control related aspects of the latent space can be very difficult.

## 3. Implementation Steps (for Approach 1)

To integrate the full $\beta$-TCVAE framework, we need to modify our model's loss computation and update the training loop in the Lightning module.

### Step 1: Modify `SeqVaeTeb` in `vae_teb_model.py`

We will add a new method to the `SeqVaeTeb` class to compute the decomposed loss terms using MWS.

```python
# Add this method to the SeqVaeTeb class in model/vae_teb_model.py

def compute_tc_loss(self, z, mu, logvar, dataset_size):
    """
    Computes the β-TCVAE loss components using Minibatch Weighted Sampling.
    
    Mathematical Foundation:
    - I_q(z;n) = E[log q(z|x,y) - log q(z)]
    - TC(z) = E[log q(z) - log ∏_j q(z_j)]  
    - DW_KL = E[log ∏_j q(z_j) - log p(z)]

    Args:
        z (torch.Tensor): Latent samples from the posterior. Shape: (batch_size, seq_len, latent_dim)
        mu (torch.Tensor): Mean of the posterior. Shape: (batch_size, seq_len, latent_dim)
        logvar (torch.Tensor): Log-variance of the posterior. Shape: (batch_size, seq_len, latent_dim)
        dataset_size (int): The total number of samples in the training dataset.

    Returns:
        dict: A dictionary containing mi_loss, tc_loss, and dw_kl_loss.
    """
    batch_size, seq_len, latent_dim = z.shape
    
    # Reshape for minibatch processing: (batch_size * seq_len, latent_dim)
    z_flat = z.reshape(batch_size * seq_len, latent_dim)
    mu_flat = mu.reshape(batch_size * seq_len, latent_dim)
    logvar_flat = logvar.reshape(batch_size * seq_len, latent_dim)
    
    # Numerical stability: clamp log variance
    logvar_flat = torch.clamp(logvar_flat, min=-10, max=10)
    
    num_samples = batch_size * seq_len
    
    # Log-density of the posterior q(z|x,y) for each sample
    log_q_z_xy = -0.5 * (math.log(2 * math.pi) + logvar_flat + 
                         (z_flat - mu_flat).pow(2) / logvar_flat.exp()).sum(dim=1)

    # MWS: Compute log q(z_i) under all encoders q(z|x_j,y_j) in the minibatch
    # Shape: (num_samples, num_samples, latent_dim)
    z_expanded = z_flat.unsqueeze(1)  # (num_samples, 1, latent_dim)
    mu_expanded = mu_flat.unsqueeze(0)  # (1, num_samples, latent_dim)
    logvar_expanded = logvar_flat.unsqueeze(0)  # (1, num_samples, latent_dim)
    
    # Compute log densities: log q(z_i | x_j, y_j) for all i,j pairs
    _log_q_z = -0.5 * (math.log(2 * math.pi) + logvar_expanded + 
                       (z_expanded - mu_expanded).pow(2) / logvar_expanded.exp())
    # Sum over latent dimensions: (num_samples, num_samples)
    _log_q_z = _log_q_z.sum(dim=2)

    # MWS estimator for log q(z): logsumexp over j, then normalize
    log_q_z = torch.logsumexp(_log_q_z, dim=1) - math.log(dataset_size * num_samples)

    # MWS estimator for log ∏_j q(z_j): sum over dimensions of individual logsumexp
    # For each dimension d: log q(z_d) = logsumexp_j(log q(z_d | x_j, y_j)) - log(N*M)
    log_q_z_marginals = torch.logsumexp(_log_q_z.unsqueeze(2) - logvar_expanded, dim=1) - math.log(dataset_size * num_samples)
    log_prod_q_z_j = log_q_z_marginals.sum(dim=1)

    # Log-density of the prior p(z) = N(0, I)
    log_p_z = -0.5 * (math.log(2 * math.pi) + z_flat.pow(2)).sum(dim=1)

    # Decomposed loss terms (sign convention: positive = penalty)
    mi_loss = (log_q_z_xy - log_q_z).mean()
    tc_loss = (log_q_z - log_prod_q_z_j).mean()
    dw_kl_loss = (log_prod_q_z_j - log_p_z).mean()

    return {
        'mi_loss': mi_loss,
        'tc_loss': tc_loss,
        'dw_kl_loss': dw_kl_loss
    }

```

    ```python
    # In LightSeqVaeTeb._common_step
    def _common_step(self, batch, batch_idx):
        y_st, y_ph, x_ph, y_raw = batch.fhr_st, batch.fhr_ph, batch.fhr_up_ph, batch.fhr

        forward_outputs = self.model(y_st, y_ph, x_ph)
        
        # Standard reconstruction loss
        recon_loss_dict = self.model.decoder.compute_loss(
            linear_output=forward_outputs['linear_output'],
            raw_mu_predicted=forward_outputs['mu_pr'],
            raw_logvar_predicted=forward_outputs['logvar_pr'],
            target_fhr_st=y_st,
            target_fhr_ph=y_ph,
            target_raw_signal=y_raw
        )
        recon_loss = recon_loss_dict['total_decoder_loss']

        # β-TCVAE decomposed loss
        tc_loss_dict = self.model.compute_tc_loss(
            z=forward_outputs['z'],
            mu=forward_outputs['mu_post'],
            logvar=forward_outputs['logvar_post'],
            dataset_size=self.hparams.dataset_size
        )

        # Combine losses
        total_loss = (
                      recon_loss +
                      self.hparams.alpha * tc_loss_dict['mi_loss'] +
                      self.hparams.beta * tc_loss_dict['tc_loss'] +
                      self.hparams.gamma * tc_loss_dict['dw_kl_loss']
                     )

        loss_dict = {
            'total_loss': total_loss,
            'reconstruction_loss': recon_loss,
            **tc_loss_dict
        }
        return loss_dict
    ```

3.  **Update Logging**: In `training_step` and `validation_step`, log the new loss components.

    ```python
    # In training_step and validation_step

    ```

## 4. Evaluation Metrics for FHR Disentanglement

### 4.1. Physiological Factor Identification

For FHR signal analysis, we expect to identify the following disentangled factors:

**Primary Physiological Factors:**
- **Baseline FHR**: Normal heart rate level (110-160 bpm)
- **Variability Patterns**: Beat-to-beat variations and long-term trends
- **Accelerations**: Temporary increases in FHR (>15 bpm for >15 seconds)
- **Decelerations**: Temporary decreases in FHR
- **Periodic Patterns**: Oscillations related to fetal movement or uterine contractions

**Secondary Factors:**
- **Gestational Age Effects**: Developmental changes in FHR patterns
- **Maternal Factors**: Heart rate, movement, position effects
- **Signal Quality**: Noise, artifacts, signal dropout

### 4.2. Modified MIG for Sequential Physiological Data

Since FHR data doesn't have explicit ground truth factors like dSprites, we adapt the evaluation approach:

```python
    }

def compute_fhr_clinical_features(fhr_raw):
    """
    Extract clinically relevant FHR features for evaluation.
    
    Args:
        fhr_raw: Raw FHR signal (batch, sequence_length)
        
    Returns:
        torch.Tensor: Clinical features (batch, num_features)
    """
    batch_size = fhr_raw.shape[0]
    features = []
    
    for i in range(batch_size):
        signal = fhr_raw[i].cpu().numpy()
        
        # Basic statistics
        baseline = np.median(signal)
        variability = np.std(signal)
        
        # Accelerations (>15 bpm increase for >15 seconds at 4Hz = 60 samples)
        accelerations = count_accelerations(signal, baseline, threshold=15, min_duration=60)
        
        # Decelerations
        decelerations = count_decelerations(signal, baseline, threshold=15, min_duration=60)
        
        # Frequency domain features
        freq_features = compute_frequency_features(signal)
        
        sample_features = [
            baseline, variability, accelerations, decelerations,
            *freq_features
        ]
        features.append(sample_features)
    
    return torch.tensor(features, dtype=torch.float32)
```

```python
def assess_factor_interpretability(model, test_loader, device):
    """
    Assess how well individual latent factors correspond to interpretable FHR features.
    """
    model.eval()
    
    # Collect latent codes and corresponding clinical features
    latent_codes = []
    clinical_features = []
    
    with torch.no_grad():
        for batch in test_loader:
            y_st, y_ph, x_ph = batch.fhr_st.to(device), batch.fhr_ph.to(device), batch.fhr_up_ph.to(device)
            y_raw = batch.fhr.to(device)
            
            outputs = model(y_st, y_ph, x_ph)
            z = outputs['z']  # (batch, seq_len, latent_dim)
            
            # Average over sequence length for global representation
            z_global = z.mean(dim=1)  # (batch, latent_dim)
            
            # Extract clinical features
            clinical = compute_fhr_clinical_features(y_raw)
            
            latent_codes.append(z_global.cpu())
            clinical_features.append(clinical)
    
    latent_codes = torch.cat(latent_codes, dim=0)
    clinical_features = torch.cat(clinical_features, dim=0)
    
    # Compute correlation matrix between latent factors and clinical features
    interpretability_scores = {}
    
    for i in range(latent_codes.shape[1]):
        latent_factor = latent_codes[:, i]
        
        correlations = []
        for j in range(clinical_features.shape[1]):
            clinical_factor = clinical_features[:, j]
            corr = torch.corrcoef(torch.stack([latent_factor, clinical_factor]))[0, 1]
            correlations.append(abs(corr.item()))
        
        # Find the clinical feature most correlated with this latent factor
        max_corr_idx = np.argmax(correlations)
        max_correlation = correlations[max_corr_idx]
        
        interpretability_scores[f'latent_{i}'] = {
            'max_correlation': max_correlation,
            'best_clinical_match': max_corr_idx,
            'all_correlations': correlations
        }
    
    return interpretability_scores
```

## 5. Comparison: Standard TEB vs. $\beta$-TCVAE-TEB

### 5.1. Theoretical Differences

| Aspect | Standard TEB | $\beta$-TCVAE-TEB |
|--------|--------------|------------------|
| **Latent Structure** | Entangled representations | **Disentangled representations** |
| **Prior Distribution** | $p(\mathbf{z}\|\mathbf{y})$ (conditional, learned) | $p(\mathbf{z}) = \mathcal{N}(0, \mathbf{I})$ (fixed, simple) |
| **Information Control** | Controls overall information flow | **Independent control of MI, TC, and KL terms** |
| **Generation Capability** | Conditional generation only | **Unconditional + controllable generation** |
| **Interpretability** | Limited factor interpretation | **Explicit factor disentanglement** |
| **Transfer Entropy** | Direct proxy via KL divergence | **Preserved in Index-Code MI term** |

### 5.2. Expected Benefits for FHR Analysis

**Enhanced Clinical Interpretability:**
- Individual latent factors may correspond to specific physiological processes
- Clearer separation of maternal vs. fetal factors
- Better identification of pathological vs. normal patterns

**Improved Robustness:**
- Disentangled representations are more robust to domain shifts
- Better generalization across different gestational ages and maternal conditions
- Reduced sensitivity to data collection artifacts

**Advanced Analysis Capabilities:**
- Factor-wise sensitivity analysis
- Controllable synthetic FHR generation for data augmentation
- Clearer understanding of UP→FHR information transfer mechanisms

### 5.3. Computational Considerations

**Memory Complexity:**
- Standard TEB: $O(B \cdot S \cdot D)$ where $B$=batch size, $S$=sequence length, $D$=latent dim
- $\beta$-TCVAE-TEB: $O(B^2 \cdot S^2 \cdot D)$ due to MWS computation

**Training Time:**
- $\beta$-TCVAE-TEB requires approximately 2-3x longer training time
- Additional hyperparameter tuning for $\alpha$, $\beta$, $\gamma$

**Memory Optimization Strategies:**
```python
def compute_tc_loss_memory_efficient(self, z, mu, logvar, dataset_size, chunk_size=64):
    """
    Memory-efficient computation of TC loss using chunked processing.
    """
    batch_size, seq_len, latent_dim = z.shape
    num_samples = batch_size * seq_len
    
    if num_samples <= chunk_size:
        return self.compute_tc_loss(z, mu, logvar, dataset_size)
    
    # Process in chunks to reduce memory usage
    z_flat = z.reshape(num_samples, latent_dim)
    mu_flat = mu.reshape(num_samples, latent_dim)
    logvar_flat = logvar.reshape(num_samples, latent_dim)
    
    mi_losses, tc_losses, dw_kl_losses = [], [], []
    
    for i in range(0, num_samples, chunk_size):
        end_idx = min(i + chunk_size, num_samples)
        
        z_chunk = z_flat[i:end_idx]
        mu_chunk = mu_flat[i:end_idx]
        logvar_chunk = logvar_flat[i:end_idx]
        
        # Reshape back and compute losses for chunk
        chunk_z = z_chunk.unsqueeze(0).unsqueeze(0)
        chunk_mu = mu_chunk.unsqueeze(0).unsqueeze(0)
        chunk_logvar = logvar_chunk.unsqueeze(0).unsqueeze(0)
        
        chunk_losses = self.compute_tc_loss(chunk_z, chunk_mu, chunk_logvar, dataset_size)
        
        mi_losses.append(chunk_losses['mi_loss'])
        tc_losses.append(chunk_losses['tc_loss'])
        dw_kl_losses.append(chunk_losses['dw_kl_loss'])
    
    return {
        'mi_loss': torch.stack(mi_losses).mean(),
        'tc_loss': torch.stack(tc_losses).mean(),
        'dw_kl_loss': torch.stack(dw_kl_losses).mean()
    }
```

```python
def update_beta_schedule(self, current_epoch, max_epochs):
    """
    Implement β annealing for stable β-TCVAE training.
    """
    if current_epoch < self.hparams.beta_anneal_epochs:
        # Linear annealing from 0 to target value
        progress = current_epoch / self.hparams.beta_anneal_epochs
        self.hparams.beta = self.hparams.beta_start + progress * (self.hparams.beta_end - self.hparams.beta_start)
    else:
        self.hparams.beta = self.hparams.beta_end
    
    # Log current β value
    self.log('beta_current', self.hparams.beta, on_epoch=True)
```

```python
        }
    
    def _common_step(self, batch, batch_idx):
        """Enhanced common step with β-TCVAE loss computation."""
        
        # Standard forward pass
        y_st, y_ph, x_ph, y_raw = batch.fhr_st, batch.fhr_ph, batch.fhr_up_ph, batch.fhr
        forward_outputs = self.model(y_st, y_ph, x_ph)
        
        # Standard reconstruction loss
        recon_loss_dict = self.model.decoder.compute_loss(
            linear_output=forward_outputs['linear_output'],
            raw_mu_predicted=forward_outputs['mu_pr'],
            raw_logvar_predicted=forward_outputs['logvar_pr'],
            target_fhr_st=y_st,
            target_fhr_ph=y_ph,
            target_raw_signal=y_raw
        )
        recon_loss = recon_loss_dict['total_decoder_loss']
        
        # β-TCVAE decomposed loss
        tc_loss_dict = self.model.compute_tc_loss(
            z=forward_outputs['z'],
            mu=forward_outputs['mu_post'],
            logvar=forward_outputs['logvar_post'],
            dataset_size=self.hparams.dataset_size
        )
        
        # Combine losses with current β values
        total_loss = (
            recon_loss +
            self.hparams.alpha * tc_loss_dict['mi_loss'] +
            self.hparams.beta * tc_loss_dict['tc_loss'] +
            self.hparams.gamma * tc_loss_dict['dw_kl_loss']
        )
        
        # Enhanced loss dictionary
        loss_dict = {
            'total_loss': total_loss,
            'reconstruction_loss': recon_loss,
            'mi_loss': tc_loss_dict['mi_loss'],
            'tc_loss': tc_loss_dict['tc_loss'],
            'dw_kl_loss': tc_loss_dict['dw_kl_loss'],
            
            # Individual reconstruction components
            'mse_loss': recon_loss_dict['mse_loss'],
            'nll_loss': recon_loss_dict['nll_loss'],
            
            # Disentanglement quality metrics
            'total_correlation_magnitude': tc_loss_dict['tc_loss'].detach(),
            'mutual_information_magnitude': tc_loss_dict['mi_loss'].detach()
        }
        
        return loss_dict
    
    def on_train_epoch_start(self):
        """Update β annealing at the start of each epoch."""
        super().on_train_epoch_start()
        self.update_beta_schedule(self.current_epoch, self.trainer.max_epochs)
    
    def training_step(self, batch, batch_idx):
        """Enhanced training step with additional metrics."""
        loss_dict = self._common_step(batch, batch_idx)
        
        # Log all components
        self.log('train/total_loss', loss_dict['total_loss'], on_step=True, on_epoch=True, prog_bar=True)
        self.log('train/recon_loss', loss_dict['reconstruction_loss'], on_step=True, on_epoch=True)
        self.log('train/mi_loss', loss_dict['mi_loss'], on_step=True, on_epoch=True)
        self.log('train/tc_loss', loss_dict['tc_loss'], on_step=True, on_epoch=True, prog_bar=True)
        self.log('train/dw_kl_loss', loss_dict['dw_kl_loss'], on_step=True, on_epoch=True)
        
        # Track disentanglement quality
        self.log('train/tc_magnitude', loss_dict['total_correlation_magnitude'], on_epoch=True)
        
        return loss_dict['total_loss']
```

## 7. Conclusion

By replacing the standard KL-divergence term with the $\beta$-TCVAE decomposition, we can directly penalize the statistical dependencies between the latent dimensions of our FHR signal representation. This encourages the model to learn a more disentangled and interpretable latent space, where individual latent factors may correspond to distinct physiological phenomena (e.g., baseline, accelerations, decelerations). This enhanced interpretability is crucial for clinical applications and can lead to more robust models for downstream tasks.

### 7.1. Key Advantages of $\beta$-TCVAE-TEB Integration

**Theoretical Benefits:**
- **Explicit disentanglement control**: Direct penalty on factor dependencies via Total Correlation
- **Principled decomposition**: Clear separation of Index-Code MI, TC, and Dimension-wise KL terms
- **Preserved transfer entropy**: Information flow measurement maintained through Index-Code MI term
- **Enhanced interpretability**: Individual latent factors correspond to physiological processes

**Practical Benefits for FHR Analysis:**
- **Clinical interpretability**: Latent factors may map to clinically relevant FHR characteristics
- **Controllable generation**: Enable synthetic FHR data generation for augmentation and analysis
- **Robust representations**: Better generalization across different patient populations and conditions
- **Factor-wise analysis**: Ability to analyze individual physiological contributions independently

### 7.3. Expected Challenges and Mitigation Strategies

**Challenge 1: Computational Complexity**
- *Issue*: $O(B^2 S^2 D)$ complexity for MWS computation
- *Mitigation*: Implement chunked processing, reduce batch sizes, use gradient accumulation

**Challenge 2: Hyperparameter Sensitivity**
- *Issue*: Need to tune $\alpha$, $\beta$, $\gamma$ simultaneously
- *Mitigation*: Start with literature values ($\alpha=\gamma=1$, $\beta=6$), use systematic grid search

**Challenge 3: Training Stability**
- *Issue*: Potential posterior collapse or mode collapse during training
- *Mitigation*: Implement careful $\beta$ annealing, monitor TC magnitude, use gradient clipping

**Challenge 4: Evaluation Complexity**
- *Issue*: No ground truth factors for FHR data like synthetic datasets
- *Mitigation*: Develop domain-specific metrics based on clinical FHR features

### 7.4. Success Criteria and Validation

**Quantitative Metrics:**
- **Reconstruction Quality**: Maintain or improve MSE and NLL compared to standard TEB
