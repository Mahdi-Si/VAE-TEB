# Final iteration of the lag-attentive CFS Transformer

Reviewed and audited 2026-11-27 against the working tree, saved configurations, the trained S8 checkpoint, the full package test suite, and local evaluation data. This is a design decision for a fresh training run.

**Keep Fable’s two-stage design. Train a probabilistic fetal heart rate (FHR) forecaster from FHR history, freeze it, then train a small uterine activity (UA) correction. Make the correction deterministic first.** Both branches still predict a distribution over future FHR. This gives an exact, paired log-score difference on fully observed scored blocks, and an exact return to the base forecast when UA is unavailable. The stochastic transfer latent remains a controlled experiment. A wider Transformer is justified only if it beats a strong FHR-only forecaster on identical targets and history.

The first engineering change is upstream of the network. The one-sided filter bank is causal in the signal it receives, but today that signal can contain monotone cubic bridges toward later valid samples and retrospective quality flags. On one local healthy shard, changing a single future valid sample moved an earlier bridged FHR value by $4.29$ bpm on a geometric anchor, and **even among anchors surviving S8’s forecast mask**, $64/84{,}256$ sit inside the $4$ s bridge right-slope lookahead window: perturbing one raw FHR sample strictly after the anchor by $+5$ bpm moved the bridged prefix on $28/84{,}256$ surviving anchors by up to $2.55$ bpm and shifted recomputed causal scattering at the scored anchor by up to $0.093$ training SD. Establish **stored-timeline prefix equivalence** from raw samples through both forecasts before spending the production budget—and keep it distinct from **recorded-clock online causality**, because canonical shards shift UA earlier by $\mathrm{up\_shift\_secs}=-20$ s relative to FHR.

### Architecture and training as built

`SeqVaeLagAttnTrfCfs` composes the CFS warm-up/anchor and target-domain mixins with the conv-Transformer network. Each target step passes through channel selection, an availability-aware adapter, a gated causal convolution stem with a $21$-step receptive field, and six global causal attention blocks at width $128$, four heads and feed-forward width $512$. The blocks use rotary positions, RMSNorm and SwiGLU. The target state $h^y_t$ produces a diagonal Gaussian prior over $64$ latent coordinates. Its auxiliary clock (`_prior_clock`) encodes zero UA with the source adapter. At fixed geometry and inference settings, the base branch is already invariant to the **values** of UA, though its parameters can still move during joint training—and freezing the base in S8 had to freeze `source_adapter` as well to keep `_prior_clock` constant. Removing `_prior_clock` in the redesigned Phase A/B architecture breaks that legacy coupling cleanly. [Model composition](nets/model.py) · [Transformer](../lag_attn_transformer_rws/nets/model.py) · [CFS forward](../lag_attn_cfs/nets/causal_inputs.py)

The active source path uses the position-wise UA adapter. The three source Transformer blocks described by the optional encoder settings do not run on this arm. The prior mean supplies the query. Each of four heads attends over $38$ positions, lags $0$ through $37$ ($148$ s), using content, learned lag embeddings, biases and entmax. The posterior fuses each head’s source summary with $h^y_t$ and gates an update to the prior. A shared horizon decoder expands the base and full latents into $30\times76$ future FHR locations and log scales, with a learned own-channel persistence skip. The two stochastic paths can share their noise. The latent never observes the future target. [Heads](../lag_attn/nets/heads.py) · [Decoder](../lag_attn/nets/decoders.py) · [Forward and readouts](DESIGN.md)

Dense anchors are $t=134,\ldots,269$. Training tiles this interval at stride $15$ with a phase determined by recording, segment, epoch and seed. Each segment has nine or ten real training anchors and ten allocated slots. Validation uses all $136$ before quality exclusions. S8’s scattering novelty threshold ($0.05$) removes early cells, including the entire $30$-step horizon for two kept scattering channels (declared `fhr_st` indices $30$ and $31$, which still occupy decoder output capacity without ever receiving loss gradients). Fast phase channels stop after five steps. Of $2{,}280$ possible cells, S8 scores $696$ scattering and $620$ phase cells before observation masking. There is **no scored scattering cell at the first horizon step** ($\tau=0$ has $0$ scattering and $44$ phase cells). Training quality weights further restrict contributing anchors, including a $0.9$ horizon-coverage floor. ### Future dependence in signal repair

The quality builder makes invalid spans into monotone cubic bridges between valid endpoints, using Fritsch–Carlson slopes estimated over a $4$ s ($\mathrm{bridge\_slope\_s}=4.0$, $16$-sample) window outside each gap. It transforms the repaired signals into scattering and phase features. Several quality rules also inspect future samples (`up_quality` lookahead up to $180$ s; `FhrBit.JUMP` padding $0.5$ s). In the current default source path, validity enters the UA adapter. FHR quality acts on target scoring, while the target trunk and persistence skip consume already computed coefficients. A downstream flag or a forward fill applied after the feature bank cannot remove future dependence already present in the coefficients. [Bridge and rules](../../hdf5_dataset/signal_quality.py) · [Quality builder](../../hdf5_dataset/new_pipeline/create_new_pipeline.py) · [Forward inputs](../lag_attn_cfs/task.py) · [Target-quality reduction](../lag_attn_rws/task.py)

On the same shard, selecting the per-segment FHR invalid-bit definition via `fhr_repair_exact` and inspecting raw index $240+16t$ across all $136$ dense anchors gives two concrete mechanisms:
- **Anchor inside a bridged gap.** **$3{,}921/102{,}816$ geometric anchors ($3.81\%$) ended inside an FHR invalid span with a later valid sample, versus $0/84{,}256$ surviving S8 anchors.** In the first affected geometric example, perturbing only the first subsequent valid sample by $+5$ bpm with flags fixed changed the bridge at the anchor by $4.289$ bpm. On the UA stream, **$299/84{,}256$ ($0.35\%$) surviving S8 anchors** ended inside a bridged UA invalid span (where S8 relies on `source_running_gap_keys` removal rather than causal pre-repair).
- **Bridge right-slope lookahead on surviving S8 anchors.** Even when the anchor itself is valid and passes S8’s full forecast mask, **$64/84{,}256$ surviving S8 anchors** follow an FHR invalid span that ended within the $4$ s right-slope window before raw index $240+16t$, so the bridge inside that earlier gap reads raw FHR samples strictly **after** the anchor. Perturbing a single future raw FHR sample ($b+16 > 240+16t$) by $+5$ bpm with flags fixed moved the bridged FHR at or before the anchor on **$28/84{,}256$ surviving anchors**. In the largest case (segment $379$, anchor $t=179$, raw index $3{,}104$, gap $[3{,}035,3{,}089)$, perturbing sample $3{,}105$ by $+5$ bpm), the bridged prefix shifted by up to **$2.546$ bpm**, which shifted recomputed `complex128` causal scattering at scored anchor $t=179$ by up to **$0.0927$ training SD** (channel $0$) and phase by up to **$0.0291$ training SD** (channel $13$).

This directly proves future leakage into the feature inputs of **scored S8 anchors** even with fixed flags, before adding retrospective quality-flag transitions. A full raw-prefix replay starting upstream of quality detection and repair is the required end-to-end gate.

## 4. Data changes before retraining

1. **Make an inference-time input stream.** Begin with available pre-repair samples and verify their provenance, including the adaptor, resampling, interpolation, sanitization and missing-value handling. Apply a prefix-only quality rule and causal fill on each stream before computing either feature or raw tokens. Carry last-valid value and age, or a learned missing token with an explicit validity flag. A gap with no observed prefix gets its own cold-start state. Keep any retrospective quality labels in the target-score or descriptive path. Test ordinary prefixes, cold starts, gap interiors, endpoints and quality transitions. Replace all later raw samples and recompute the entire prefix. Inputs, flags, skips and both forecasts through the anchor must agree within a declared numerical tolerance. Declare the clock convention explicitly: keep $\mathrm{up\_shift\_secs}=-20$ s only for stored-timeline parity with S8 and standalone `CouplingIRF`, and verify **recorded-clock** non-anticipativity ($\mathrm{up\_shift\_secs}=0$) before making any live clinical-forecasting claim. [Preprocessing availability and continuous-history tasks](../CFS_SCATTERING_PHASE_FIX_TASKS.md)
2. **Transform continuous stretches once, then crop.** Preserve the integer harmonic operator (`phase_operator='integer_harmonic_v1'`), `leg_alignment='envelope'`, channel order, complex filter/phase-leg history and decimation grid. Use the PyTorch build path (`CausalTorchBank`) and pass both flags explicitly—its defaults remain `'ratio_power_v0'` and `'none'`, and the float64 numpy reference showed minor float16 rounding drift against stored shards in the registered study. Verify float16 round-trip parity before running rebuilds or synthetic plants. Compare streaming or chunked computation to a long uninterrupted reference at multiple chunk boundaries and offsets. Store per-channel availability, the quality mask, valid raw counts and the true context length for each crop. Retain warm-up at real starts and resets. If only shards survive, reconstruct pre-repair raw samples by GUID and timestamp, verify overlaps bitwise, deduplicate and split on gaps or conflicts. Join contiguous stretches only. The local coverage audit found $78\%$ of consecutive kept pairs at a $1{,}200$ s stride. The share with enough history for $40$ minutes is a separate count. [Continuous-history specification](../CFS_SCATTERING_PHASE_FIX_TASKS.md) · [Torch build bank](../../hdf5_dataset/causal_scattering_torch.py) · [Coverage audit](../../tmp/lag-research/tables/AUDIT.md)
3. **Separate context from scoreable output.** For comparison with S8, retain a central $300$-step scoring crop plus preceding context and $30$ future labels where available. A $600$-step context means up to $600$ past steps at each anchor. Allocate the prefix and forecast suffix explicitly. At most $270$ dense anchors fit **inside** a $300$-step crop if its horizon must also fit and the $134$ floor can be removed. Startup, label validity, left-context policy and segment gaps reduce that number. Sample a fixed quota of anchors from the central region during training. Evaluate on deduplicated recording timestamps and provide counts by history length and time to delivery. Changing the context must not silently change the scored cohort.
4. **Version the label and support definition.** Start with S8’s $76$ channels, novelty starts and horizon stops for a matched comparison, while explicitly deciding whether to drop the two unscored slow scattering channels (declared `fhr_st` indices $30,31$) or relax S8’s $0.05$ novelty threshold (which also leaves $\tau=0$ with zero scored scattering cells). ## 5. Final architecture and objective

### Phase A — FHR-only probabilistic base

Use the existing causal conv-Transformer trunk and feed the decoder $h^y_t$ directly, omitting the legacy `_prior_clock` zero-UA path so Phase A has zero architectural dependence on `source_adapter`. Add the trailing raw FHR level, log variability and causal validity to the scattering/phase input. Give the forecast mean a direct linear skip from the last $K$ valid-aware FHR lags, with an own-channel term and a low-rank cross-channel term. Try $K=8$, rank $16$, against AR(38) and a longer-history skip. Preserve re-entry after gaps without treating filled values as measurements. Keep horizon embeddings, FiLM refinement and one horizon attention block initially. Profile the decoder at the actual training anchor count. Its output is a mean $\mu^b_{t,\tau,c}$ and an innovation scale $s^b_{t,\tau,c}>0$. Add the persistence correction to the mean only.

For the initial matched density, use per-channel AR(1) residuals, fixed Student-$t$ degrees of freedom $\nu=8$ and $|\phi_c|\leq0.95$. With $r_\tau=y_\tau-\mu_\tau$, the innovation on consecutive scored cells is $e_\tau=r_\tau-\phi_c r_{\tau-1}$. Start each channel at its own first scored horizon $\tau^0_c$ with $e_{\tau^0_c}=r_{\tau^0_c}$ (matching the `started = (cell_mask.cumsum(-2) > 0)` reset in `losses.py`). The triangular transformation has unit Jacobian. Here $s$ is a Student-$t$ **scale** and its innovation variance is $s^2\nu/(\nu-2)$. The level error still contributes to each innovation when $\phi_c$ is near one, but a persistent location bias is attenuated after the first scored step. Score forecast level MSE alongside NLL to expose this tradeoff. [Likelihood implementation](../lag_attn_rws/nets/losses.py)

**Gaps require an explicit likelihood policy.** The current routine propagates a variance across missing steps and evaluates the next innovation with that scale. This integrates missing independent Gaussian innovations exactly. A weighted sum of independent Student-$t$ innovations generally has another distribution. For a first production comparison, use the complete observed scored blocks as the primary exact Student-$t$ log-score population. For incomplete blocks, train and report a separately labelled composite of observed one-step transition scores where both residuals are observed, plus each observed channel-start score. Also report their coverage and the denominator by quality stratum. If incomplete-block likelihood is a primary requirement, implement an exact Gaussian masked-AR comparator or a coherent joint Student-$t$ block with an explicit shared scale mixture and exact observed marginals. Those are different predictive models and need calibration on the same observed support.

Train Phase A with the normalized feature NLL, a level loss such as normalized MSE, and a separately normalized auxiliary raw FHR loss. A positive level-loss weight is a candidate to test against zero. Use a training-split scaling convention so that an extra $60$ raw targets cannot dominate by unit or multiplicity. An auxiliary marginal-scale head is also possible, but its score would be a composite training objective. A separately predicted marginal scale is not automatically the marginal of the AR density. Select on base skill and calibration and report the unweighted feature log score separately from every auxiliary term.

### Phase B — add a source-specific mean correction

Freeze **all** Phase A weights, running state, normalizers, density parameters and raw heads. Run the base in evaluation mode even when the trainer enters training mode. Only the new residual parameters are in the optimizer. Start its output weights at zero while leaving the upstream path trainable. The base distribution and the initial full distribution should coincide. The legacy freeze helper contains an allow-list of legacy module names. Adapt the behavior and test actual parameter identities for the new class. [Freezing and evaluation mode](../lag_attn_cfs/task.py)

Use trailing raw UA level above a causal resting-tone estimate (with optional causal amplitude scaling to handle toco staircase quantization and probe-gain variation), slope, valid fraction and selected fast UA coefficients. Record the definition and support of each. Choose raw-only and feature-only controls before promoting their combination. The old intervention on slow channels supports reducing the source representation, but does not measure the benefit of new raw tokens. Begin with a small causal UA stem and eight disjoint lag bands over $0,\ldots,75$ stored steps. For example, use inclusive bands $[0,1],[2,3],[4,7],[8,14],[15,23],[24,35],[36,51],[52,75]$. Keep the stem local and publish its raw receptive field, including any trailing baseline, filters and stacked layers. A state at lag $\ell$ already summarizes earlier samples. A band index alone cannot specify the raw signal’s response delay.

One implementation uses the following residual, with $M$ the causal UA availability pattern, $U^0$ a prespecified value-null with the same flags, and $h=\operatorname{stopgrad}(h^y_t)$.

$$
d_b=f_b(U_b,M_b,h)-f_b(U_b^0,M_b,h),\qquad
\mu^f=\mu^b+a(M)\sum_b G_b(h)d_b,\qquad s^f=s^b .
$$

Here $U_b$ denotes the band representation computed from the source stream. Recompute its null through the same encoder, using identical nuisance inputs and stochastic masks, or use a bias-free source map with the same zero identity. Set $a(M)=0$ when the admissible source history is empty. The subtraction removes a correction depending only on FHR and the mask; FHR can still modulate a genuine UA response. Additive contributions sum exactly in **mean-output space**. Correlated bands and the nonlinear log score preclude an additive gain or KL interpretation. Apply the residual to the auxiliary raw FHR forecast and train that output in Phase B if raw response timing is to be read from it.

Train on every eligible contraction age. Use own-UA predictive loss. Optionally add a donor displacement penalty, for example the mean of $[(\mu^\pi-\mu^b)/s^b]^2$ on common scored cells with a fixed lower scale bound. This penalty is zero at output equality. It controls location shifts under donor UA and has a different value and gradient from the old centered donor NLL. Select its weight, including zero, using **absolute own skill subject to a donor-equivalence constraint**. Maximizing own-minus-donor alone rewards making donor predictions worse. Use the same donor assignments for paired comparisons, drawn from distinct recordings with comparable stage/time, subgroup and coverage where available. Note that `val_dataloader` in the shared data module uses `shuffle=False`, where $71.5\%$–$81.1\%$ of adjacent rows share a GUID; never rely on naive in-batch rolling for validation donors—build an explicit GUID-disjoint derangement (or recording-stratified batching) and report pairing failures. Unrelated UA can resemble a real contraction. The model cannot be expected to identify donor identity from every plausible history.

### Fable’s stochastic residual as one ablation

A candidate transfer distribution is $q(z\mid h,U)=\mathcal N(m,\operatorname{diag}v)$ with $d_z=8$ initially and a location update $a(M)G(h)z$. A unit reference prior makes the per-anchor rate $R=\tfrac12\sum_j(m_j^2+v_j-1-\log v_j)$ easy to report. It includes reference mismatch and can encode target context. It has no direct physiological information interpretation. Freezing the base addresses gradient absorption. Source centering and a bypass address value-null behavior.

If $q=\mathcal N(0,I)$ while $G\ne0$, a sampled residual has covariance $GG^\top$. The mean correction vanishes, yet the predictive distribution changes. Implement an explicit bypass for missing and declared value-null UA, including **every draw and the reported density**. Compare stochastic and deterministic outputs with the same inputs, masks, model-selection rule and compute. Log the rate in nats per anchor. If the reconstruction uses nats per cell, put both terms on the same denominator, e.g. $(\sum_i \mathrm{NLL}_i+\beta\sum_iR_i)/\sum_i n_i$, where $n_i$ counts scored cells and $i$ ranges over contributing anchors. The original $\beta$ values are candidates only under a stated reduction.

With $K$ draws of the per-block decoder likelihood, the marginal score estimator is

$$
\widehat\ell_K=\operatorname{logsumexp}_{k=1}^{K}\log p(y_{\mathrm{obs}}\mid z_k)-\log K.
$$

Sum the log density over jointly scored cells **before** the sample reduction. $\mathbb E[\widehat\ell_K]\le\log p(y_{\mathrm{obs}}\mid Y,U)$ under valid sampling and a normalized component density. A particular Monte Carlo result can exceed the truth. Compare $K=8,32,128$ on fixed development recordings with repeated noise seeds and likelihood-weight effective sample size. A targeted increase in $K$ should change the gain by much less than its recording uncertainty before this score can select an architecture. Promote the latent only for reproducible gain or calibration relative to the deterministic residual at matched specificity.

## 6. Capacity, compute and a bounded experiment

Spend capacity on FHR history first. For the existing rotary attention and SwiGLU layout, a trunk block has approximately $4d^2+3d\,d_{\mathrm{ff}}$ matrix weights. These are **block-stack** estimates. Input layers, skip, source path and decoder are extra.

| Candidate | Width $d$ | Blocks | $d_{\mathrm{ff}}$ | Approximate block-stack weights |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| S8-width control | $128$ | $6$ | $512$ | $1.57$ M |
| Small | $192$ | $8$ | $768$ | $4.72$ M |
| Medium | $256$ | $10$ | $1024$ | $10.49$ M |
| Large, conditional | $384$ | $12$ | $1536$ | $28.31$ M |

A **medium total around $16$ M is a candidate budget**. Count unique parameters after instantiation. Full-prefix attention compute includes a term proportional to $T^2d$ in addition to the projections proportional to $Td^2$. Longer crops also increase the number of decoded anchors unless it is held fixed. A forecast of GPU-hours based solely on parameters or an assumed twofold mixed-precision speedup is unreliable.

## 7. What counts as a successful final iteration

Lock the minimum useful effect, acceptable regression and one primary endpoint for each scientific claim **before confirmation**. Use selection data to check power and set numeric margins. For a recording $g$ and arm $a$, sum unweighted block log scores over identical eligible anchors and divide by that recording’s total observed scored cells. Compare arms per recording, then average recordings equally. Publish pooled-cell results as a secondary analysis. Require the same eligible rows for a paired contrast. Declare empty bands, shortened support, missing donors and excluded recordings. Bootstrap paired recordings or patients, account for donor reuse if estimating over donor draws, and use simultaneous intervals or a prespecified hierarchy for multiple primary horizon comparisons.

| Gate | Measurement and action |
| --- | --- |
| **G1 · Forecast** | Compare normalized level MSE for scattering and phase, raw FHR MAE in bpm, and primary unweighted feature NLL. Report horizon bands $1$–$5$, $6$–$10$, $11$–$20$ and $21$–$30$ steps on their scored cells. Require a paired improvement over the strongest eligible FHR-only comparator on the prespecified primary endpoint and no material loss on the other primary block. Set the allowed regression on development. Empty cell sets are undefined. |
| **G2 · Calibration** | Check innovation PIT and central prediction intervals using the actual Student-$t$ CDF and scale. Report $50\%$, $80\%$ and $95\%$ coverage with interval widths by block/horizon and minimum support. A candidate tolerance is $\pm0.02$ absolute coverage error on the pooled $95\%$ interval. | **G3 · UA gain** | Let $g_o=\ell_{\mathrm{own}}-\ell_b$ and $g_\pi=\ell_{\mathrm{donor}}-\ell_b$ in nats per scored cell. ## 8. Research behind the choices

| Decision | What the primary source contributes |
| --- | --- |
| Fit the FHR history first and quantify incremental UA value | Kalajdzievski et al. describe a bottleneck for predictive information from a second stream and a previously modeled target. ChronosX shows that modular covariate additions to a pretrained forecaster can work in other domains. These motivate the comparison. The local held-out experiment decides it. [Transfer Entropy Bottleneck](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.16607) · [ChronosX, AISTATS 2025](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v258/arango25a.html) |
| Measure predictive gain with a matched proper log score | Gneiting and Raftery formalize scoring a predictive distribution. Xu et al. explain how usable predictive information depends on the predictor family. A positive finite-model log-score difference alone cannot identify conditional mutual information, transfer entropy or a physiological causal effect. [Proper scoring rules](https://sites.stat.washington.edu/raftery/Research/PDF/Gneiting2007jasa.pdf) · [Predictive information under computational constraints](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.10689) |
| Fit long linear controls and a simple causal response model | Zeng et al. demonstrate competitive direct linear forecasts on other time-series benchmarks. For intrapartum data, Vargas-Calixto et al. compare a nonlinear static map plus dynamic response with a linear system. This supports including a small response baseline and testing it directly on this forecast horizon. [Linear time-series baselines](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.13504) · [Nonlinear dynamic FHR response, CinC 2022](https://cinc.org/archives/2022/pdf/CinC2022-268.pdf) |
| Use controlled perturbations to assess timing | Wibral et al. derive delay identification for a specified transfer measure with appropriate self-history conditioning. Jain and Wallace show that different attention patterns can make similar predictions in NLP. The direct evidence for S8 comes from its fixed-profile and shift interventions. [Measuring information-transfer delays](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23468850/) · [Attention experiments](https://aclanthology.org/N19-1357/) |
| Interpret donor swaps as controls | Tansey et al.’s holdout randomization test requires draws from the conditional feature distribution for its inferential guarantee. Matching donors by stage and coverage is a useful falsification experiment. Its bootstrap interval describes that donor protocol. [Holdout randomization test](https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.00645) |
